Market

The job market right now

  • AI as a skill is the #1 thing employers look for. It should come through naturally, not as a resume line.
  • AI progress has slowed and settled. The program didn't get easier: AI raises the bar. One engineer is expected to do maybe two or three people's work. "100x" is marketing.

Context

Where platform engineering fits in a business

  • Everything starts with money: buyers pay for software; that revenue pays employees, cloud compute, security. You work somewhere in that chain.
  • B2B = business to business. B2C = business to consumer. Customer = the buyer; user = whoever uses it (people, apps, agents).
Thatch → Slack → GitHub → Cursor → New York Times (each sells to the next)

Who sends Thatch's requests?

  • HR admins and employees in the benefits portal (browser/app)
  • HR/payroll software like Gusto checking budgets and deductions
  • Insurers (Blue Cross, Aetna, Cigna) checking enrollment, Group ID, Member ID
  • AI agents (partners' or Thatch's own) and internal data-analytics apps
  • Same "requests" that drove HPA scaling in Containers Session 12, and the request path from Assignment 11.
  • Even non-software companies run software (a 100-year-old water-fountain maker, Target's inventory app).

Self-test

Quiz: check yourself

Answer each out loud first, then open the answer. Score yourself out of 10.

1. An interviewer asks, "What are you most excited about this year?" You say "AI" and stop. Why is that weak, and what makes it strong?

Show answer

"AI" alone is a claim; a strong answer is proof. Follow with one or two concrete things you built or automated, the harness you used, and what you learned (where it failed, how you checked it). If the interviewer doesn't feel real excitement backed by real use, they won't believe it.

2. "Knowledge is cheap." Explain it with an example, and say what it means for how you present Terraform and Kubernetes.

Show answer

Anyone can get any skill on demand with AI, so a skill list alone isn't an asset. Photoshop used to be a resume line; now you ask Claude to retouch the photo. "Terraform, Kubernetes" is the new "I know Photoshop." Sell what you built and decided: the multi-account environment, failures you debugged, trade-offs, and how AI helped.

3. A classmate says, "My company can't use AI because of HIPAA." How would Marcel answer, and what AWS service backs him up?

Show answer

Compliance isn't a capability ceiling; the company just hasn't implemented compliant AI yet. Healthcare companies already work with OpenAI and Anthropic. AWS Bedrock runs models with enterprise privacy and security and can be HIPAA-eligible when set up properly. The real limit today is the physical world.

4. What's the difference between a model and a harness? Give a pair for each of the four big companies.

Show answer

The model is the brain; the harness is the tool around it that reads files, runs commands and acts. Claude → Claude Code; GPT → Codex; Gemini → Antigravity; Grok → Grok CLI. The same model can feel very different in a weak vs a strong harness.

5. Security asks, "Why don't we self-host an open-weight model instead of paying for an API?" What are the trade-offs?

Show answer

Full data control, but very expensive and you own the operations. Pros: data stays in your infra, no per-token bill. Cons: a 1M-token context needs ~700 GB+ RAM and $1M+ of GPUs (or rented GPU servers), and you handle scaling and uptime. Middle ground: a managed, compliant service like Bedrock. Chinese open models are often off-limits for US enterprises anyway.

6. Why keep your AI memories in a private GitHub repo instead of just moving them into ChatGPT?

Show answer

A repo is model-agnostic and future-proof. Memory locked in one vendor dies when you switch (exactly what's happening with Claude → GPT). Context in Git works with any harness, has history, and can be shared between agents. Keep it private and personal, not in the school org.

7. A Thatch pod's HPA scales up because requests spiked. Name at least four kinds of clients that could be behind those requests.

Show answer

"Requests" rarely means only people in a browser. HR admins and employees in the portal; payroll software like Gusto calling an API; insurers' apps checking enrollment and member IDs; AI agents; internal analytics jobs. Knowing who calls you helps you reason about traffic, auth, rate limits and blast radius.

8. B2B vs B2C, and customer vs user. Why does it matter to a platform engineer?

Show answer

B2B sells to businesses, B2C to consumers. The customer pays; the user uses (a person, app or agent). In a B2B chain your outage hits other companies' systems and contracts, so reliability, integrations and SLAs weigh heavily. Customer revenue pays for your compute and your salary.

9. In the Project X simulation, who plays back-end dev, front-end dev and product manager, and what exactly is your job?

Show answer

AI agents play those roles; you're the platform engineer (plus security). You build the infra from scratch in Terraform with separate dev/staging/prod accounts, EKS, ECR, secrets, ACM, autoscaling, then CI/CD (Argo CD), monitoring, logging and on-call. No infra base is provided.

10. "Doesn't AI make you 100x more productive? Shouldn't we just hire fewer people?" Give a balanced answer.

Show answer

"100x" is marketing; realistically a strong engineer with AI does two or three people's work, which raises the bar rather than removing the job. AI still needs a human to direct, review and fix it. Companies pay more for people who prove this: recent grads $105K–$180K base, median ~$145K. Show both the leverage and the limits, plus how you check AI output (multi-model review with sub-agents).
