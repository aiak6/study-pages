Summary

The whole session in 5 lines

  1. Certificates are a nice bonus, not proof. Interviews are won by how you explain things.
  2. CKAD: ~16 auto-graded tasks, pass at 66% (64% on the free retry). Aim for ~14 done right.
  3. Hard tasks often weigh only 4–5% — skip them if time is short. Easy ones weigh 8–9%.
  4. Core skill = understand the task, find the right Kubernetes docs page, copy the YAML, adapt it.
  5. Use logic only at the high level. Inside the YAML, just follow Kubernetes rules (e.g. the field is serviceAccountName).
1

Are certifications worth it?

Do certificates prove you're a good engineer?

Show answer

No. More and more people have them, and hiring managers have seen many weak candidates with 3–5 certs.

  • Some people buy certs or pass by exam dumping (memorizing leaked questions).
  • Good engineers interview at a few places and get hired fast. Weak candidates stay in the market longer, so interviewers meet a lot of them.
  • People rarely question whether your experience is real. They question how good you are.

So why get one at all?

Show answer
  1. You learn a lot while preparing.
  2. It's a nice bonus on the resume.

Think of it as a bonus, not the main layer. The school has loosened the cert requirement for this reason.

Can we find a job without a certificate?

Show answer

Yes. It's not required.

Any extra benefit of CKAD specifically?

Show answer

You practice without AI — AI isn't allowed in the exam. It builds real foundation skills.

Resume & interviews: what actually helps

What matters on the resume?

Show answer

The overall impression: "who is this person and what's their experience like?"

  • Experience, companies, the summary, and what's written under each job.
  • Certificates on top = you look good "from all angles".
  • AI experience is now a separate, valued thing on a resume.

Will my resume get through the ATS / LinkedIn screening systems?

Show answer

It should. Resumes will be covered later in the course.

Do certificates help in the interview itself?

Show answer

Close to zero. What helps is the knowledge you gained, e.g. if they give you a hands-on task.

How many interview rounds are there? Do they give hands-on tests?

Show answer

Usually about 3 rounds (the resume screen doesn't count). Could be more or fewer.

Most companies don't give hands-on tests like the CKAD lab. They judge you by how you speak and what you describe.

CKAD: format & scoring

ItemDetail
Tasks~16
Pass score66% first attempt, 64% second attempt
RetrySecond attempt is free
GradingFully automated — no human
FeedbackNone during the exam (black box)
Target~12 tasks fully right to pass; aim for 14

How is it graded?

Show answer

By a computer. It checks whether a test passes, not what you tried.

One missed detail — wrong namespace, wrong file name, wrong field — can void the whole task.

You might feel you got 91% and actually get 70% because of small details.

Do I see my score while working, like the practice lab's progress bar?

Show answer

No. It's a black box until the final score. Otherwise everyone would just fix their mistakes.

Can I skip tasks? Do they have to be done in order?

Show answer

You can skip. Each task is separate.

Hard tasks often weigh only 4–5%, easy ones 8–9%. If a hard, low-weight task is eating your time, skip it.

Example: skip 4 tasks worth 8% each = 32% lost → still 68% if the rest is right.

How similar are the school's practice labs to the real exam?

Show answer

Very similar tasks, different details (names, namespaces, wording).

  • Real exam might say "update the YAML file, then apply it" instead of "update the live deployment".
  • Don't memorize one-to-one — a small change will break you. Understand what you're doing.
  • Lab had about a year without major updates. Fundamentals (service accounts, resources, probes) haven't changed; Ingress/Gateway and traffic splitting may be outdated.

How fast should I be?

Show answer

While learning: ignore the clock, even if one task takes an hour.

When close to ready: finish the practice lab in ~90 minutes (not the full 2 hours), because the real exam's slight variations will slow you down.

Fast but without errors — and take a moment to review each task.

4

Exam technique: docs first

What does every task really test?

Show answer
  1. Understanding what the concept is.
  2. Finding the right docs page and applying it.

Memorize commands or use the docs?

Show answer

Docs. Memorizing patch commands is harder. Stick to one approach: search docs → copy YAML → adapt.

The exam scenario is often the exact example from the docs (sometimes even the same image).

All official Kubernetes docs are available during the exam.

How do I find the right page fast?

Show answer
  • Prefer pages with /tasks in the URL — they have copy-paste YAML. /concepts pages mostly explain.
  • Use Ctrl+F / Cmd+F for the exact field, e.g. httpGet or serviceAccount.
  • Learn in advance which page you need for each topic, so you don't hunt during the exam.

The flow for every task

Show answer
read carefully → search docs → copy YAML → paste & edit → kubectl apply → verify (if time)

Read for every detail: namespace, file name, field names.

Edit live or export to a file?

Show answer

Either works — practice both.

kubectl edit deploy <name> -n <ns>                      # fastest
kubectl get deploy <name> -n <ns> -o yaml > task1.yaml   # then edit + apply

If you export, you can delete the whole status: section. If the task says a YAML file already exists, edit that file — the instructor missed this himself in the demo.

Worked example: readiness probe (task 1)

Task: add a readiness probe to a deployment (/healthz, port 5000, initialDelaySeconds 10, periodSeconds 3)

Show answer

Docs: search ReadinessProbe → open the /tasks page (Configure Liveness, Readiness and Startup Probes) → Ctrl+F httpGet.

The first httpGet example is a liveness probe. The docs say a readiness probe is the same — only the field name changes.

spec:
  containers:
  - name: app
    image: ...
    readinessProbe:
      httpGet:
        path: /healthz
        port: 5000
      initialDelaySeconds: 10
      periodSeconds: 3
  • Goes inside spec.containers (per container).
  • Delete the example's httpHeaders unless asked.
  • Writing livenessProbe instead of readinessProbe = task fails.

Readiness vs liveness?

Show answer

Same way to configure. Understand the difference: readiness = ready to get traffic; liveness = still alive, restart if not.

Worked example: ServiceAccount (tasks 2 & 3)

Task: "Update deployment alfred to use existing ServiceAccount alfred-sa"

Show answer

Mental picture: update the Pods in a Deployment to use some ServiceAccount.

spec:            # Deployment spec
  template:
    spec:        # Pod spec ← here
      serviceAccountName: alfred-sa
      containers:
      - ...
  • Goes in the Pod template spec, at the same level as containers, not inside it.
  • There are two spec: blocks in a Deployment — use the second one (the Pod's).
  • Double-check the name spelling.

The task says "update deployment" — why are we changing the Pod?

Show answer

A Deployment is just a grouping that manages Pods. You can't attach an identity to a Deployment.

Same idea as "update the Auto Scaling Group to a new OS" really means updating the EC2s.

Deployment, StatefulSet, DaemonSet, CronJob → they all run Pods, so the change goes into the Pod spec.

Why serviceAccountName and not just serviceAccount?

Show answer

Kubernetes' rule, not logic. The kube-apiserver only understands that exact field name. It could have been called anything.

Use logic at the high level (what does the task want?). Stop using logic inside the YAML — just follow the docs.

There are so many hidden assumptions — how do I get the big picture?

Show answer

There is no big picture — there are many small pictures (service accounts, probes, limits…).

Being confused is normal. Each small picture needs its own cycle: confused → work through it → it clicks.

That's what the 1–2 weeks of prep time is for. The lab is an exam simulation, not the lesson.

Task 3: "Fix the errors in the logs of deployment hyperv"

Show answer
  1. Check Pod logs (the Deployment makes the Pods): kubectl logs <pod> -n <ns>
  2. Error: Forbidden — the default ServiceAccount can't list pods.
  3. If you don't set a ServiceAccount, the Pod uses default in its namespace.
  4. kubectl get sa -n <ns> → there's hyperv-sa (its RoleBinding allows get/watch/list on pods).
  5. Add serviceAccountName: hyperv-sa to the Pod spec in the given YAML file → kubectl apply -f.
  6. Verify: logs no longer show errors.

Exam tasks usually want one fix, not five. You don't need to check the RoleBinding in the real exam.

Hard topics & how to practice

DifficultyTopics
Medium-hardNetworkPolicy (which pod gets which rule), Ingress/Gateway, traffic splitting
Mostly executionServiceAccount, resource limits, env vars, secrets, probes, CronJobs, security context, rollback, API deprecation, NodePort service, building an image

How do I practice the hard ones?

Show answer

Have AI create scenarios in your own cluster and quiz you until it clicks (e.g. NetworkPolicy: allow A→B, B→A).

The school lab has one scenario per task; AI can make unlimited variations — change names so you're not just memorizing.

Can AI help me learn the docs?

Show answer

Yes, during practice (not the exam). Install the Claude or ChatGPT browser extension and ask it to find the best docs page and explain how it found it.

AI is lazy — it grabs the first result. Ask it to find 2 more options and compare.

You can also screenshot a task and ask "what am I doing here and why?"

Do I use the school cluster for AI practice?

Show answer

No — use your own so you have full control.

Cheapest: Google Cloud GKE — the control plane is free, you pay only for nodes (likely a couple of dollars, maybe free tier). EKS is more expensive.

Ask Claude/Codex to spin it up, practice, then terminate it.

Tips for specific tasks

Show answer
  • Secrets → Pod: the page "Distribute Credentials Securely Using Secrets" has env and volume examples.
  • Security context: "Configure a Security Context" (tasks page) has the exact example.
  • CronJob: "Running Automated Tasks with a CronJob" — one of the easiest, often 8%.
  • Rollback: kubectl rollout undo or a quick YAML change.
  • Logs task: the answer will be obvious in the Pod logs.

Buying, timing & CKA vs CKAD

How much is it, and where do I buy?

Show answer

~$445 full price, ~$250 with a discount (they run 20–40% off, likely around Cyber Monday / Black Friday).

Buy directly from the Linux Foundation. Sign up to their emails for coupons.

You can schedule within 1 year of buying. If budget is tight, it's fine to wait.

Should I buy the training bundle?

Show answer

No — waste of time and money. Use: the Udemy course + school practice labs + AI practice + killer.sh.

What's killer.sh?

Show answer

Free simulator included when you buy the exam. A bit harder than the real exam, but the environment is almost identical (servers, copy-paste mechanics). Definitely use it.

When should I prepare?

Show answer

December. Project X will be done, resumes ready, and while waiting for interviews you'll have downtime.

1–2 weeks of focused practice should be enough for CKAD.

CKA vs CKAD — which is harder / more valuable?

Show answer

CKA is harder — about 30% extra after CKAD; lots of overlap. Less time too.

CKA adds cluster admin (etcd, control plane setup) that most jobs don't need — EKS manages that.

Value: the instructor isn't sure one is worth more. Gap is like AWS Developer Associate vs SysOps/Solutions Architect Associate — not like Practitioner vs Associate.

CKA practice lab: published (per staff in the session).

The External Secrets lab is confusing — should I dig in?

Show answer

Not too deep. The lab uses a fictional simulation (e.g. a namespace pretending to be a vault store).

You'll implement External Secrets for real in Project X, and it'll make more sense then.
