Concepts

The main ideas, from zero

Ephemeral vs persistent: why Postgres lost its table

  • Ephemeral means short-lived. Containers are meant to die and be replaced.
  • Files a container writes without a volume (/app, /tmp) sit on the node's root EBS disk. A replacement pod starts fresh from the image.
  • Stateless app (the web app): losing local files is fine, because its real data lives in the database.
  • Stateful app (Postgres, MySQL, MongoDB, WordPress): losing files means losing data.
  • Persistent storage gives the pod its own disk with its own lifetime. The pod dies, the disk stays, and the new pod mounts it again.
Where the files liveContainer restartsPod is replaced
Inside the container, no volumeStarts freshStarts fresh
Persistent volumeFiles remainReplacement reuses the disk
Pods come and go. Your data should not.
  • Why companies care: banks and healthcare must keep records for 5–10 years, even after the app is gone. Separate storage makes that possible.

The three objects: PVC, StorageClass, PV

ObjectWhat it isWho makes itScope
PVC (PersistentVolumeClaim)The request: size, access mode, StorageClass nameDeveloperYour namespace
StorageClassThe template: provisioner (EBS CSI), disk type (gp3), encryption, reclaim policy, binding modeDevOps, onceWhole cluster
PV (PersistentVolume)Kubernetes's record of the real diskCreated automaticallyWhole cluster
EBS volumeThe actual disk holding the filesThe provisioner, in AWSOne AZ
developer applies PVC → StorageClass → provisioner creates EBS disk → Kubernetes records it as a PV → PVC bound to PV (one-to-one) → pod mounts the claim at a folder
  • Binding is strictly one claim to one PV. Kubernetes matches size, access mode and StorageClass. No match, no storage.
  • StorageClass is like the launch template for an Auto Scaling group (Scale Session 09): set once, reused for every new disk.
  • Manual provisioning (admin makes disks and PVs by hand) is old and painful. Dynamic provisioning (just the claim) is what you'll use at work.
The claim is the request. The PV is the record. EBS holds the files.

Pending is normal: WaitForFirstConsumer

  • The class StorageClass unit6-ebs uses volumeBindingMode: WaitForFirstConsumer.
  • The disk is created only when a pod uses the claim, so the disk lands in the same availability zone as the pod.
  • So a claim with no pod sits at Pending, and that's fine. The other mode, Immediate, creates it right away.
  • Pending after the pod exists is a real problem. Read the events: kubectl describe pvc postgres-data-unit6.

Mounting: two names that must match

  • volumes: (pod level) names a volume and points it at the claim with claimName.
  • volumeMounts: (container level) uses that same name and a mountPath.
  • Postgres keeps its files in /var/lib/postgresql/data, so that folder becomes the EBS disk.

Access modes and reclaim policy

Access modeMeans
ReadWriteOnce (RWO)One node reads and writes; pods on that node can share it. The usual mode for EBS.
ReadOnlyManyMany nodes read, none write
ReadWriteManyMany nodes read and write. Needs EFS; EBS attaches to one node at a time
ReadWriteOncePodOne pod in the whole cluster
ActionWhat happens to the disk
Replace the podClaim and disk remain
Delete the claim, policy DeleteDisk and data deleted (dev/test default)
Delete the claim, policy RetainDisk stays until an admin deletes it; charges continue (production default)
Delete the namespaceIts claims are deleted; their reclaim policies apply
Persistent storage is not a backup.

Deployment vs StatefulSet

DeploymentStatefulSet
Pod namesRandom hash, new on every replacementStable and ordered: postgres-0, postgres-1
StorageReplicas point at the same claimOne claim per pod, made from volumeClaimTemplates
ScalingAll at once, any orderOne at a time, in order
ReplacementNew nameSame name, same claim
Good forStateless apps; a 1-replica DB worksDatabases and other stateful apps
  • A StatefulSet does not set up replication or backups.
  • Reality check: most teams run production databases on RDS, outside the cluster. A shared cluster means someone else's bad limits can hurt your database, and a disk alone doesn't give you backups, failover, upgrades or multi-AZ. Same reasoning as moving Postgres to RDS in Scale Session 05–06.

Jobs and CronJobs: work that finishes

Job → creates a pod → task runs → exit 0 = Complete (non-zero = failed attempt) CronJob (on schedule) → creates a Job → creates a pod → ... → sleeps until next time
  • A Deployment keeps something running forever. A Job runs a task until it succeeds, then stops.
  • backoffLimit: 2 = up to two retries after failures, then the Job is marked failed.
  • restartPolicy: Never = each retry gets a new pod, so each failed pod's logs stay readable.
  • ttlSecondsAfterFinished: 300 = Job and its pods are deleted 300 s after finishing. Logs go with the pod, so read them first.
  • The class Job: move "create the tables" out of app.py into a Job that runs schema.sql (from a ConfigMap) with psql. CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS makes reruns safe.
  • Kubernetes doesn't make the Deployment wait for the Job. You check Complete, then deploy the app.
  • CronJob = a Job on a schedule. Five fields: minute, hour, day of month, month, day of week. "0 2 * * *" = every day at 02:00 (UTC with timeZone: "Etc/UTC"). Same cron as your Telegram bot (Foundations Class 13).
  • Eldiiar's work example: a daily CronJob counts the days until a GitHub token expires and warns at two weeks.
A Deployment keeps serving. A Job finishes. A CronJob makes a new Job on a schedule.

Hands-on

Redo the demo yourself

Before you start: kubectl pointed at the class cluster, the course repo cloned, and your terminal in 06-storage-and-jobs/. Set your namespace once: export NAMESPACE=<your-namespace>. For part C you also need Docker running, the AWS CLI logged in, and an ECR repository (Containers Session 03).

A. Lose the table (ephemeral)

1

Run Postgres with no volume and create the table.

kubectl -n "$NAMESPACE" apply -f postgres-ephemeral.yaml
kubectl -n "$NAMESPACE" rollout status deployment/unit6-postgres --timeout=300s
kubectl -n "$NAMESPACE" exec -i deployment/unit6-postgres -- sh -ec \
  'psql -U "$POSTGRES_USER" -d "$POSTGRES_DB" -v ON_ERROR_STOP=1' < schema.sql   # creates trades

CREATE TABLE.

2

Replace the pod, then look for the table. This is the "pod crashed" moment.

kubectl -n "$NAMESPACE" delete pod -l app=unit6-postgres
kubectl -n "$NAMESPACE" wait --for=condition=Ready pod -l app=unit6-postgres --timeout=300s
kubectl -n "$NAMESPACE" exec deployment/unit6-postgres -- sh -ec \
  'psql -U "$POSTGRES_USER" -d "$POSTGRES_DB" -c "SELECT side, price FROM trades;"'

relation "trades" does not exist. The table was in the old container's files.

B. Keep the table (persistent)

3

Check the StorageClass, stop the old Postgres, create the claim.

kubectl get storageclass unit6-ebs                                    # made by the admin, once
kubectl -n "$NAMESPACE" scale deployment/unit6-postgres --replicas=0
kubectl -n "$NAMESPACE" apply -f pvc.yaml
kubectl -n "$NAMESPACE" get pvc postgres-data-unit6

STATUS Pending. Normal: no pod uses it yet (WaitForFirstConsumer).

4

Deploy Postgres with the claim mounted.

kubectl -n "$NAMESPACE" apply -f postgres-persistent.yaml   # also defines the Service unit6-db
kubectl -n "$NAMESPACE" rollout status deployment/unit6-postgres --timeout=300s
kubectl -n "$NAMESPACE" get pvc,pv

the claim Bound to a new PV you never wrote. The pod takes a little longer to start, because AWS is creating the disk. In the EC2 console under Volumes: a new gp3 1 GiB volume.

5

Create the table, save a trade, replace the pod, read it back.

kubectl -n "$NAMESPACE" exec -i deployment/unit6-postgres -- sh -ec \
  'psql -U "$POSTGRES_USER" -d "$POSTGRES_DB" -v ON_ERROR_STOP=1' < schema.sql
kubectl -n "$NAMESPACE" exec deployment/unit6-postgres -- sh -ec \
  'psql -U "$POSTGRES_USER" -d "$POSTGRES_DB" -c "INSERT INTO trades (side, price) VALUES ('\''buy'\'', 150.00);"'
kubectl -n "$NAMESPACE" delete pod -l app=unit6-postgres
kubectl -n "$NAMESPACE" wait --for=condition=Ready pod -l app=unit6-postgres --timeout=300s
kubectl -n "$NAMESPACE" exec deployment/unit6-postgres -- sh -ec \
  'psql -U "$POSTGRES_USER" -d "$POSTGRES_DB" -c "SELECT side, price FROM trades;"'

buy | 150.00. New pod name, same disk, same data.

C. Create the tables with a Job (the after-class demo)

6

Make sure the DB Service exists. This is what broke both live demos.

kubectl -n "$NAMESPACE" get svc unit6-db   # missing? re-apply postgres-persistent.yaml
7

Put schema.sql in a ConfigMap and run the Job.

kubectl -n "$NAMESPACE" create configmap unit6-schema \
  --from-file=schema.sql --dry-run=client -o yaml | \
  kubectl -n "$NAMESPACE" apply -f -          # create-or-update in one line
kubectl -n "$NAMESPACE" apply -f schema-job.yaml
kubectl -n "$NAMESPACE" wait --for=condition=complete job/unit6-schema --timeout=180s
kubectl -n "$NAMESPACE" logs job/unit6-schema

job.batch/unit6-schema condition met, and in the logs relation "trades" already exists, skipping (the table from step 5 is kept).

8

Build and push the revised app (no table creation at startup). Run from 06-storage-and-jobs/, not from app/. Follow README section 3 for the exact steps.

export REVISED_IMAGE=<account>.dkr.ecr.<region>.amazonaws.com/<repo>:<tag>   # your ECR repo
# log in with the command your ECR repository shows (aws ecr get-login-password | docker login ...)
docker build --platform linux/amd64 -t "$REVISED_IMAGE" app/   # quotes around the variable!
docker push "$REVISED_IMAGE"

the push finishing with a digest.

9

Deploy the revised app only after the Job is Complete, then open it. Put your image URI in the revised app's Deployment file (README section 3), apply it, then:

kubectl -n "$NAMESPACE" rollout status deployment/unit6-investment-app
kubectl -n "$NAMESPACE" port-forward <from the README>   # then open http://localhost:8080

the investment app working; buy and sell go through.

If it goes wrong

ErrorMeaning
Job logs: could not translate host name "unit6-db"The Service is missing. Apply postgres-persistent.yaml, then kubectl delete job unit6-schema and apply it again
App gives HTTP 500The tables don't exist because the Job never completed. Fix the Job; the app didn't need a restart after-class
docker push: image does not exist locallyThe shell didn't expand the variable. Wrap it in double quotes: "$REVISED_IMAGE"
docker build: app not foundYou're inside app/. Go up to 06-storage-and-jobs/
Claim still Pending after the pod existskubectl describe pvc postgres-data-unit6 and read the Events (e.g. the StorageClass name doesn't exist)
Delete is stuck in TerminatingNormal for storage. The claim waits for its pod to stop using it. Give it a few minutes

Clean up

The EBS disk costs money while the claim exists. unit6-ebs uses Delete, so removing the claim removes the disk.

kubectl -n "$NAMESPACE" delete job unit6-schema --ignore-not-found
kubectl -n "$NAMESPACE" delete -f postgres-persistent.yaml -f pvc.yaml --ignore-not-found
kubectl -n "$NAMESPACE" get pvc,pv          # your claim and PV should be gone

Then check EC2 → Volumes that your volume is gone, and delete the revised app Deployment you applied in step 9.

The claim and the mount:

# pvc.yaml (shape)
apiVersion: v1
kind: PersistentVolumeClaim
metadata:
  name: postgres-data-unit6
spec:
  accessModes: ["ReadWriteOnce"]
  storageClassName: unit6-ebs        # ties the claim to the template
  resources:
    requests:
      storage: 1Gi

# postgres-persistent.yaml (the part that matters)
containers:
- name: postgres
  volumeMounts:
  - name: database-files             # same name as below
    mountPath: /var/lib/postgresql/data
volumes:
- name: database-files
  persistentVolumeClaim:
    claimName: postgres-data-unit6   # selects the claim

StorageClass settings (made once by the admin): provisioner ebs.csi.aws.com, type: gp3, encrypted, reclaimPolicy: Delete, volumeBindingMode: WaitForFirstConsumer, allowVolumeExpansion (false here).

Job and CronJob:

apiVersion: batch/v1
kind: Job
metadata:
  name: unit6-schema
spec:
  backoffLimit: 2                   # two retries, then failed
  ttlSecondsAfterFinished: 300      # auto-delete 5 min after finishing
  template:
    spec:
      restartPolicy: Never          # new pod per retry
      containers:
      - name: schema
        image: postgres:16          # supplies psql
        # command + DB connection + schema ConfigMap mount: see schema-job.yaml
---
apiVersion: batch/v1
kind: CronJob

  schedule: "0 2 * * *"             # every day at 02:00
  timeZone: "Etc/UTC"

Emphasis

What the instructor stressed

  • Developers shouldn't touch disks. DevOps sets up the StorageClass once; developers just write a claim. Self-service, no Jira ticket.
  • Watch the cost anyway. EBS is cheap, but audit monthly or quarterly for abandoned volumes and teach teams to clean up.
  • Retain in production, Delete in dev/test.
  • Don't run production databases in Kubernetes. Use RDS. You still need StatefulSets for interviews and certifications.
  • Don't write cron expressions by hand. Ask AI or crontab.guru.
  • Use AI to debug: that's how both instructors found the missing Service.
  • Install Freelens for a visual view of the cluster.
  • Gemini notebooks (NotebookLM-style) can turn a video into slides or a podcast if you learn visually.

Self-test

Check yourself

1. You apply pvc.yaml and k get pvc shows Pending. A teammate says the storage is broken. Are they right?

Show answer

Not yet. With WaitForFirstConsumer, no disk is made until a pod uses the claim, so it lands in the pod's AZ. Pending after the pod exists is a real problem: run k describe pvc and read the Events.

If this one was shaky, reread "Pending is normal."

2. You scale your Postgres Deployment (one RWO EBS claim) from 1 to 3 replicas. What goes wrong, and what should you use instead?

Show answer

Replicas on other nodes can't attach the disk, and database replicas each need their own disk anyway. Use a StatefulSet: volumeClaimTemplates gives postgres-0, postgres-1, postgres-2 a claim each. Replication is still your job (or use RDS).

If this one was shaky, reread "Deployment vs StatefulSet."

3. Your schema Job shows 0/1 completions, and its logs say could not translate host name "unit6-db". What's wrong, and what do you do?

Show answer

The Job can't find the database Service. Create the Service (re-apply postgres-persistent.yaml), delete the Job, and apply it again. Only deploy the app after the Job is Complete, because Kubernetes won't wait for you.

If this one was shaky, reread "Jobs and CronJobs."
